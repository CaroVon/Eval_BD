# Findings so far (2026-10-30)

Synthesis of RESULTS.md sections 1-32 for deciding the paper's claims. Numbers are from fp32
batch-invariant vLLM on A100 unless stated. Truth matrices: Qwen2.5-3B (GSM8K 1,319, MMLU-Pro
1,000 and 3,000), Qwen2.5-7B (GSM8K), Phi-3.5-mini (GSM8K, MMLU-Pro 1,000), SmolLM2-1.7B (GSM8K);
each with the Q8_0 / Q6_K / Q4_K_M / Q3_K_M llama.cpp ladder. Phi, SmolLM2 and Qwen-7B are
exploratory parents (DECISIONS.md D8, D11).

## What holds

1. **Exact screening is exact.** Lemma 3 had zero violations in all seven truth matrices: no flips
   outside D, and continuation from t\* reproduces the child output exactly. Ties are broken by
   smallest token id, as definition 2 requires.
2. **Determinism must be checked per model and mode.** Qwen2.5-3B passes all four layers in
   compiled mode; Phi-3.5 varies between processes in compiled mode (5/200 questions) and passes in
   eager mode; Qwen-7B and SmolLM2 were run eager and pass. Without batch invariance, 4.5% of Qwen
   answers flip from a batch-size change alone. bf16 logits create frequent exact ties that inflate
   D; fp32 removes them (Q8_0 |A|/|P| 0.717 -> 0.478 on the pilot).
3. **A quantitative law for screening power.** Divergence happens where the parent is nearly
   indifferent, and quantization acts as additive noise of sd sigma_q on the margin. Per-token
   divergence rate rho ~ c f_m(0) sigma_q / sqrt(2 pi) with c ~ 0.9 (24 parent x task x version
   cases, ratios 0.72-1.14, median 0.90, insensitive to how f_m(0) is estimated), so
   |A|/|P| ~ 1 - E[exp(-rho T)]. Screening power is set by the parent's own near-tie density f_m(0)
   (Phi 0.07, Qwen-7B 0.078, Qwen-3B 0.12 GSM8K / 0.19 MMLU-Pro, SmolLM2 0.124, Phi MMLU-Pro 0.164),
   the quantization noise, and output length. sigma_q per bit width is nearly the same across families
   at similar size (Q8 0.07-0.08, Q6 0.23-0.26, Q4 0.77-0.90 logits) but shrinks with scale (Qwen-7B
   25-40% lower), and is not predicted by median weight error. 50 calibration questions plus parent
   margins predict |A|/|P| (design information only).
4. **Screening power follows the parent's near-tie density, not its size.** Q8_0 |A|/|P| 0.519
   (Qwen-3B), 0.317 (Qwen-7B), 0.398 (Qwen-14B), 0.420 (Llama-3.1-8B). sigma_q falls from 3B to 7B
   but not from 7B to 14B (Q8 0.074 / 0.055 / 0.053); the 14B parent has more near-ties than 7B
   (f_m(0) 0.105 vs 0.078) and Llama-8B the most of any GSM8K parent (0.137). The margin law covers
   all 32 parent x task x version cases (ratios 0.72-1.14, median about 0.89).
   (Update 2026-10-31, RESULTS.md sec. 33-34.)
5. **Divergence is mostly harmless wording.** 60-80% of first divergences are word choices,
   10-20% punctuation; ~75% of outputs re-align with the parent within 30 tokens and most keep the
   answer. Flips come from reasoning paths that drift after an early wording change, not from
   answer-token near-ties.
6. **The decode batch decides the economics.** In batched offline evaluation the prefill/decode
   cost ratio r grows with the decode batch: r = 0.039 / 0.076 / 0.140 / 0.269 / 0.365 at 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 /
   256 sequences (3B; roughly proportional up to 128), and 0.467 for 7B at 256. Prefill throughput does
   not depend on the decode batch. At r = 0.076 the break-even audit rate is 0.11-0.19 for Q8_0 and
   Q6_K in every family, and screened certificates reach the exact H or theta at budgets 0.4-0.6 of
   full evaluation, where B1 is 2-5x looser. At r = 0.37 only Q8_0 on short-output tasks gains, at
   budgets 0.6-0.8.
   Update 2026-10-31 (sec. 32 correction): the time per decode step is nearly constant (36-42 ms
   for 3B) from batch 16 to 256, and far from the HBM limit. So r ~ B_eff x (prefill s/token) /
   (s/step) is set by a fixed per-step cost of the batch-invariant engine, not by memory bandwidth.
   Qwen-14B in fp32 still fits about 100 short GSM8K sequences in its 28k-token KV cache (measured
   r = 0.31); a small decode batch needs longer outputs or less memory, not just a larger model.
   On MMLU-Pro (long outputs) memory alone forces a mean decode batch of about 49 and r = 0.16. There
   screening beats B1 at b = 0.4 (0.117 vs 0.140) and is exact at b = 0.6 (sec. 38).
6b. **Exact screening beats every valid baseline at equal compute where it applies** (sec. 35).
   B2 (AIPW + Bernstein, parent-only predictor) is looser than B1 everywhere. B6 (CLT + Bonferroni)
   is narrower than B1 only by undercovering (simultaneous coverage 0.12-0.74). Q8_0 B1 / ours
   worst-slice ratio: 2.1-4.2 at r = 0.076, b = 0.4, and 1.5-2.1 at r = 0.365, b = 0.6 on GSM8K for
   all five parents. That meets G3's 1.5x for Q8_0, and for Q6_K at r = 0.076 for three of five parents.
   Update 2026-11-01 (sec. 41): a tighter B2 bound (exact-MGF Chernoff, worst case over flip
   placement) and the active designs B3 / B4 do not change this. B1 stays the strongest valid
   baseline on GSM8K, and tilted designs are worse than uniform for worst-slice certificates (up to
   3x). On MMLU-Pro they beat B1 by at most 0.004.

## What does not hold as planned

7. **E2/E3 at large decode batches.** With 3B and 7B models that fit 256 sequences on an 80 GB A100,
   prefill along the parent output costs about half a full generation; Q6_K and below gain nothing,
   and B1 is cheaper at the audit rates of coarse slices (pi about 0.33). On MMLU-Pro screening never
   wins at equal budget in this regime.
8. **Zero-harm design certifies nothing at tau = 0.05.** Every version has real flips.
9. **Harm-only certificates are structurally limited.** When repairs are common, any certificate
   valid for H cannot be tau-precise for theta once Rep >= tau. The net certificate
   (docs/net_certificate.md) fixes this but needs near-full audit rates. That need is mostly
   intrinsic (sec. 42): an exact Neyman-Pearson lower bound shows that any procedure at the same
   per-slice level needs 70-90% of the audit rate the net certificate uses. Examples: GSM8K Q8_0
   0.48 vs 0.65, MMLU-Pro Q6_K 0.86 vs 0.97. A joint test on the difference is not tighter.
   Flip symmetry depends on
   both model and task (Qwen about even; Phi harm-heavy on GSM8K, even on MMLU-Pro; SmolLM2 Q8_0
   repair-heavy).
10. **Cascade drafts and re-anchored verification do not rescue low bits.** Quants are independent
    perturbations of the parent. Re-anchored exact verification gains about 1.5x for Q8_0 with
    measured short-chunk costs at batch 256.
11. **MMLU-Pro is not "medium length"** for these CoT models (Qwen-3B mean 466 tokens, 7.2% truncated
    at 1,024 by long calculations), which hurts screening.

12. **Track A does not transfer to the deployed GGUF** (2026-10-31, RESULTS.md sec. 37 and 39).
    - Perplexity confirms the dequantization pipeline (parents agree with llama.cpp to 1e-4).
    - llama.cpp's quantized kernels add error beyond the weights: Qwen-3B Q8_0 changes perplexity
      by +0.24%, against +0.0002% for its dequantized weights.
    - The same bf16 weights under llama.cpp and fp32 vLLM give identical GSM8K outputs on 6 / 1,319
      questions and differ in correctness on 130 (9.9%) at equal accuracy.
    - llama.cpp is repeatable but not prefill/decode consistent (Q8_0 continuation check 217 / 400).
      Consistency returns with micro-batch 1 (400 / 400), but then prefill costs about 0.65 of decode.
    - Exact screening therefore needs a canonical, batch-invariant execution, and the certificate is
      about that execution.
    - The size of the engine gap depends on the model family (sec. 40). For Llama-3.1-8B, bf16 under
      llama.cpp reproduces vLLM on 89% of questions (Qwen-3B 0.5%), and about half of the deployed
      Q8_0 flips are Track A flips (Qwen 13%). The L3 failure is the same for both families, so it
      belongs to the engine.

## Implications for the claims (for discussion)

- The theory (certificate validity, lower bounds, structure-prediction separation) is unaffected.
- A defensible practical claim: exact screening gives several-fold tighter worst-slice certificates
  at equal compute when decoding is bandwidth-bound (small decode batches: large models, long
  outputs, limited memory) and for high bit-width versions; it does not help in large-batch offline
  evaluation of small models. r should be reported with the decode batch.
- New contributions supported by data: the margin-noise law for screening power (with its ~10%
  systematic over-prediction and a cheap predictor), its dependence on scale, the determinism
  findings (bf16 ties, per-model compile mode), the decode-batch dependence of r, and the
  net-degradation certificate with the harm-certificate impossibility.
- G3 as stated (1.5x over the strongest baseline at equal compute) is met in the small-batch regime
  for Q8_0 and Q6_K, and for Q8_0 on GSM8K at budgets 0.6-0.8 in the large-batch regime.
